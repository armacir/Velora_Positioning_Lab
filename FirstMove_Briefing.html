Year 1 — The category is born. Nobody in Tier 1–3 India buys a smart purifier yet. Most households boil water or use a basic carbon filter. You design the product and launch it.
The case

Smart water purifiers for India’s next 100 cities

About 3 million households in Tier 1, 2 and 3 cities could afford a home purifier, but almost none owns a modern one. Water quality is a daily worry; boiling is the default. Your company has the technology. You have five years to build the category and a profitable business inside it.

Each year your team decides what the product is (six attributes), who it is for and what you say (positioning), the price and whether to offer EMI instalments, where it is sold (online only, or dealers too, by city tier) and how much you spend on brand advertising and on category education (water-testing camps, demos).

You win on cumulative profit after Year 5. Running out of cash is allowed but you pay 12% interest on the overdraft.

Rules of thumb

  • Customers buy benefits, not features. A feature counts only if your target links it to something they want.
  • Brand advertising makes people know your brand. Category education makes people understand why to buy a purifier at all, and it helps every competitor.
  • People buy when people like them have already bought. Some segments won’t move until the segment before them has adopted.
  • Your positioning message must be backed by the product. Claims you can’t support cost you credibility.
  • What surprises customers today they will expect tomorrow.
  • Market research is for sale on the Team desk: a Kano study per segment (₹0.5 cr) and a market report (₹0.4 cr).
